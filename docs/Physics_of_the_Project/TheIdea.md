**The Idea of Computers-:**
Hey reader, before we bother ourselves with computers, instructions sets, processor architectures and all sorts of daunting(yet, interesting) stuff, let's address some fundamentals.
Computers at their core are simply *machines*. Now what are machines? If we strip down this term of all it's Physics and implementation level details and simply approach it from a layman's perspective, we find that *anything which minimizes human effort can be termed as a machine*. Now a wide spectrum, starting from screws to rocket engines and supercomputers fit into this definition. 
The idea for the first computer also stemmed from this. *Charles Babbage* who is widely regarded as the, *Father of Computers*, had once said *"I wish to God these calculations had been executed by steam".* Anyone who has ever practiced rigorous arithmetic, let alone Mathematics as a whole, would resound with this at once. Eventually,God granted his wish and Charles Babbage designed the *Differential Engine and Analytical Engine (the first computer)* in quick successions. 
Though never advancing beyond the design phase, the idea itself was revolutionary. The computer had components like *Mill(modern day CPU)*, *store(modern day memory)* and also could handle the I/O via *punch cards*. It could support complex operations like branching and loops. It was meant to be powered by steam (a steam engine, as it was common back in the good old days).

However a downside of this was that this system was bulky,expensive and too slow. Though still based on the mechanical system and base 10 number systems, this was far from being scalable and useful. 

During this time some other great minds like *Michael Faraday* and his likes were making path breaking discoveries in the field of electricity. Almost half a century after the design of analytical engine, did someone succeed in converting electricity into light and heat energy, and that someone was none other than *Thomas Edison*. This moment was historical in it's own terms but this was also the day, that the realm of computing saw a new dawn.

Now the fundamental limitations of engines of steam were to be countered with the strengths of electricity. However the use of electricity introduced it's own set of problems (whose solution, surprisingly existed even before the problem itself). This part though would be dealt with in the next section.
